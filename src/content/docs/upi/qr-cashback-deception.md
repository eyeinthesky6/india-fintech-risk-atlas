**ID:** UPI-006 · **Category:** Social engineering · **Status:** Research draft — independent review pending

## Summary
A fraudster may present a QR code or link as a way to receive money or cashback, while inducing the user to authorize an outgoing payment.

## Evidence from primary material
NPCI warns that entering a UPI PIN is for making a payment, not receiving one. [1]

## Potential checks and operational considerations
Consider clear transaction direction, destination identity and warnings in the payment confirmation journey; these are suggested design considerations, not universal mandates.

## Limitations and false positives
A legitimate merchant QR or genuine payment request is not inherently suspicious.

## Applicability
This article does not assert a universal mandatory control. Applicability depends on the participant's regulated role and the exact source provision.
